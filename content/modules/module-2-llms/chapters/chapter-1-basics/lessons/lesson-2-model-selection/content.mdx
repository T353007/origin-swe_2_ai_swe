# Choosing Models and Providers

> **💡** *Choosing the right LLM model and provider is like choosing both a vehicle AND a manufacturer: you need to pick the right car (GPT-4o vs GPT-4o-mini) AND the right brand (OpenAI vs Anthropic vs Google). GPT-5.2 might be the most powerful, but GPT-4o-mini will handle most tasks perfectly fine at 17x less cost. And while OpenAI is like AWS (widely used and reliable), Anthropic is like a specialized boutique (excellent quality, focused strengths). The trick is knowing when to use which model from which provider—and how to avoid getting locked into one vendor.*

## Overview

Model and provider selection are critical engineering decisions when building AI applications. The right choice balances cost, quality, latency, capabilities, and vendor considerations for your specific use case. Choosing poorly can lead to wasted costs, poor user experience, vendor lock-in, or insufficient quality.

In 2024-2025, the landscape has exploded with options across multiple providers. OpenAI offers GPT-5.2, GPT-4o, and GPT-4o-mini. Anthropic has Claude 4.5 Sonnet and Opus with extended context windows. Google offers Gemini 3 Pro with massive context windows. Each provider and model has different strengths, weaknesses, pricing, and use cases. This lesson helps you navigate this landscape effectively and implement proper abstractions to maintain flexibility.

## Main Content

### Model Comparison Factors

When selecting a model, consider these four key factors:

#### 1. Capability (Quality)

Different models excel at different task complexities:

**Premium Models (Highest Quality):**
- **GPT-5.2** (OpenAI, December 2025): Latest and most capable, best for complex reasoning, creative tasks, and quality-critical applications
- **Claude 4.5 Opus** (Anthropic): Exceptional for complex analysis, code generation, and nuanced reasoning
- **Gemini 3 Pro** (Google): Strong multimodal capabilities, excellent for complex tasks

**High-Quality Models (Best Balance):**
- **GPT-4o** (OpenAI): Excellent quality-to-cost ratio, best for most production use cases
- **Claude 4.5 Sonnet** (Anthropic): Great for coding, analysis, and general tasks
- **Gemini 2.5 Pro** (Google): Strong performance across many tasks

**Cost-Effective Models (Good for Simple Tasks):**
- **GPT-4o-mini** (OpenAI): 17x cheaper than GPT-4o, sufficient for many tasks
- **Claude 3.5 Haiku** (Anthropic): Fast and cost-effective for simpler tasks

#### 2. Cost

Token pricing varies dramatically. Here's current pricing (per 1M tokens, 2025):

**OpenAI Models:**
- **GPT-5.2**: ~$20 input, ~$90 output (premium pricing)
- **GPT-4o**: $2.50 input, $10.00 output
- **GPT-4o-mini**: $0.15 input, $0.60 output (17x cheaper than GPT-4o)

**Anthropic Models:**
- **Claude 4.5 Opus**: ~$15 input, ~$75 output
- **Claude 4.5 Sonnet**: ~$3 input, ~$15 output

**Cost Example:**
- 1M input + 500K output tokens:
  - GPT-5.2: ~$65
  - GPT-4o: ~$5.00
  - GPT-4o-mini: ~$0.45 (14x cheaper than GPT-4o)

#### 3. Latency (Speed)

Response time matters for user experience:

**Fast Models:**
- GPT-4o-mini: Very fast, excellent for real-time applications
- GPT-4o: Fast, good for most interactive applications
- Claude 3.5 Haiku: Fast and responsive

**Moderate Latency:**
- GPT-4o: Balanced speed and quality
- Claude 4.5 Sonnet: Good balance

**Slower (But Higher Quality):**
- GPT-5.2: Premium quality, slightly slower
- Claude 4.5 Opus: Highest quality, slower response

**Rule of thumb:** Mini/turbo models are faster. Larger models are slower but higher quality.

#### 4. Context Window

Maximum input size varies significantly:

**Large Context Windows:**
- **Gemini 2.5 Pro**: 1-2 million tokens
- **Gemini 3 Pro**: 1 million tokens
- **Claude 4.5 Sonnet**: 200K standard, up to 1M extended
- **Llama 4 Scout**: 10 million tokens (open source)

**Standard Context Windows:**
- **GPT-5.2**: 400,000 tokens
- **GPT-4o**: 128,000 tokens
- **GPT-4o-mini**: 128,000 tokens
- **Claude 4.5 Opus**: 200,000 tokens

**Considerations:**
- Long context = process entire codebases, long documents
- Short context = simpler prompts, lower costs
- Context size affects both capability and cost

### Model Selection Decision Framework

Use this framework to choose the right model:

#### Step 1: Assess Task Complexity

**Simple Tasks** (classification, extraction, simple Q&A):
- Use: GPT-4o-mini or Claude 3.5 Haiku
- Why: 10-17x cheaper, sufficient quality, faster
- Examples: Sentiment analysis, keyword extraction, basic Q&A

**Medium Tasks** (summarization, translation, moderate analysis):
- Use: GPT-4o or Claude 4.5 Sonnet
- Why: Best quality-to-cost ratio
- Examples: Document summarization, code review, data analysis

**Complex Tasks** (complex reasoning, creative writing, quality-critical):
- Use: GPT-5.2, GPT-4o, or Claude 4.5 Opus
- Why: Highest quality when it matters
- Examples: Complex problem-solving, legal analysis, medical advice

#### Step 2: Consider Volume and Cost Constraints

**High Volume Applications** (1000s of requests/day):
- Start with: GPT-4o-mini
- Upgrade only if quality is insufficient
- Monitor costs and quality metrics

**Cost-Sensitive Applications**:
- Default to: GPT-4o-mini
- Test if GPT-4o is necessary for quality
- Use caching and optimization techniques

**Quality-Critical Applications** (fewer requests, quality matters):
- Use: GPT-4o or GPT-5.2
- Accept higher costs for better quality

#### Step 3: Evaluate Latency Requirements

**Real-Time/Interactive** (chatbots, live assistants):
- Prefer: GPT-4o-mini or GPT-4o
- Avoid: Largest models (GPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 Opus) unless necessary

**Batch Processing** (can wait):
- Can use: Any model
- Consider: Larger models for better quality if latency isn't critical

#### Step 4: Check Context Requirements

**Short Context** (< 10K tokens):
- Use: Any model
- Consider: GPT-4o-mini for cost savings

**Medium Context** (10K-128K tokens):
- Use: GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, or Claude models
- All handle this range well

**Long Context** (> 128K tokens):
- Use: Claude 4.5 Sonnet (up to 1M), Gemini 3 Pro (1M), or Llama 4 Scout (10M)
- Avoid: GPT-4o (limited to 128K)

### Selection Guidelines by Use Case

**General Production Applications:**
- Default: GPT-4o (best balance)
- Fallback: GPT-4o-mini if cost is critical
- Upgrade: GPT-5.2 if quality is insufficient

**Code Generation:**
- Best: GPT-4o or Claude 4.5 Sonnet/Opus
- Good: GPT-4o-mini for simpler code
- Claude models excel at code tasks

**Long Document Processing:**
- Best: Claude 4.5 Sonnet (extended context), Gemini 3 Pro
- Alternative: GPT-4o with chunking
- Avoid: GPT-4o-mini for very long documents (context limits)

**High-Volume APIs:**
- Best: GPT-4o-mini
- Why: Lowest cost, sufficient quality for most tasks
- Monitor: Quality metrics, upgrade if needed

**Creative Writing:**
- Best: GPT-5.2, GPT-4o, or Claude 4.5 Opus
- Why: Higher quality for creative tasks
- Consider: Temperature settings for creativity

**Multimodal Tasks (Images + Text):**
- Best: GPT-4o, GPT-5.2, or Gemini 3 Pro
- Why: Built-in vision capabilities
- GPT-4o-mini: Limited multimodal support

**Cost-Optimized Applications:**
- Best: GPT-4o-mini
- Strategy: Start cheap, upgrade only if quality is insufficient
- Monitor: Quality vs cost trade-offs

### Cost Comparison Calculator

Compare costs across models for your use case:

```python
def calculate_cost_comparison(
    input_tokens: int,
    output_tokens: int,
    num_requests: int = 1000
) -> dict:
    """Calculate and compare costs across models."""
    
    models = {
        "gpt-5.2": {"input_per_1m": 20.0, "output_per_1m": 90.0},
        "gpt-4o": {"input_per_1m": 2.50, "output_per_1m": 10.00},
        "gpt-4o-mini": {"input_per_1m": 0.15, "output_per_1m": 0.60},
        "claude-4.5-opus": {"input_per_1m": 15.0, "output_per_1m": 75.0},
        "claude-4.5-sonnet": {"input_per_1m": 3.0, "output_per_1m": 15.0},
    }
    
    results = {}
    for model, pricing in models.items():
        input_cost = (input_tokens / 1_000_000) * pricing["input_per_1m"]
        output_cost = (output_tokens / 1_000_000) * pricing["output_per_1m"]
        total_cost_per_request = input_cost + output_cost
        total_cost = total_cost_per_request * num_requests
        
        results[model] = {
            "cost_per_request": round(total_cost_per_request, 6),
            "total_cost": round(total_cost, 2),
            "monthly_estimate": round(total_cost * 30, 2)  # Assuming daily
        }
    
    # Sort by total cost
    sorted_results = sorted(results.items(), key=lambda x: x[1]["total_cost"])
    
    return {
        "input_tokens": input_tokens,
        "output_tokens": output_tokens,
        "num_requests": num_requests,
        "comparison": dict(sorted_results)
    }

# Example: Compare costs for 1000 requests with 1000 input + 500 output tokens
comparison = calculate_cost_comparison(
    input_tokens=1000,
    output_tokens=500,
    num_requests=1000
)

print("Cost Comparison (1000 requests, 1K input + 500 output tokens):")
for model, costs in comparison["comparison"].items():
    print(f"{model}: ${costs['total_cost']:.2f} total, ${costs['cost_per_request']:.6f} per request")
```

### Provider Abstraction and Vendor Lock-In

Avoid vendor lock-in by implementing abstraction layers that allow you to switch providers easily:

```python
from abc import ABC, abstractmethod
from typing import Optional, List
import os

class LLMProvider(ABC):
    """Abstract base class for LLM providers."""
    
    @abstractmethod
    def complete(self, prompt: str, model: Optional[str] = None, **kwargs) -> str:
        """Generate completion from prompt."""
        pass
    
    @abstractmethod
    def get_default_model(self) -> str:
        """Get default model for this provider."""
        pass

class OpenAIProvider(LLMProvider):
    """OpenAI provider implementation."""
    
    def __init__(self, api_key: Optional[str] = None):
        from openai import OpenAI
        self.client = OpenAI(api_key=api_key or os.getenv("OPENAI_API_KEY"))
        self.default_model = "gpt-4o"
    
    def get_default_model(self) -> str:
        return self.default_model
    
    def complete(self, prompt: str, model: Optional[str] = None, **kwargs) -> str:
        response = self.client.chat.completions.create(
            model=model or self.default_model,
            messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}],
            **kwargs
        )
        return response.choices[0].message.content

class AnthropicProvider(LLMProvider):
    """Anthropic provider implementation."""
    
    def __init__(self, api_key: Optional[str] = None):
        import anthropic
        self.client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=api_key or os.getenv("ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"))
        self.default_model = "claude-4-5-sonnet-20241022"
    
    def get_default_model(self) -> str:
        return self.default_model
    
    def complete(self, prompt: str, model: Optional[str] = None, **kwargs) -> str:
        response = self.client.messages.create(
            model=model or self.default_model,
            max_tokens=kwargs.get("max_tokens", 1024),
            messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}]
        )
        return response.content[0].text

# Usage: Switch providers easily without changing application code
provider = OpenAIProvider()  # Or AnthropicProvider()
result = provider.complete("Hello!")
```

### Multi-Provider Fallback

Implement fallback logic to handle provider failures:

```python
class MultiProviderClient:
    """Client with multiple provider fallbacks."""
    
    def __init__(self, providers: List[LLMProvider]):
        self.providers = providers
    
    def complete(self, prompt: str, model: Optional[str] = None) -> tuple[str, str]:
        """Try providers in order, return result and provider name."""
        for provider in self.providers:
            try:
                result = provider.complete(prompt, model)
                return result, provider.__class__.__name__
            except Exception as e:
                continue  # Try next provider
        raise Exception("All providers failed")

# Usage: Automatic fallback
client = MultiProviderClient([
    OpenAIProvider(),      # Primary
    AnthropicProvider(),   # Fallback
])

response, provider = client.complete("Hello!")
```

## Common Mistakes

#### 1. Always using the most expensive model
Using GPT-5.2 or Claude 4.5 Opus for every task wastes money. GPT-4o-mini handles most simple tasks perfectly well at 17x less cost. Start with cost-effective models and upgrade only when quality is insufficient.

#### 2. Ignoring cost differences
Model costs vary dramatically. GPT-4o-mini is 17x cheaper than GPT-4o for the same token usage. At scale, this difference is substantial. Always calculate costs for your expected volume before committing to a model.

#### 3. Not testing multiple models
Different models excel at different tasks. Claude models are excellent for code generation. GPT-4o is great for general tasks. Gemini 3 Pro has the longest context window. Test multiple models for your specific use case before deciding.

#### 4. Forgetting latency requirements
Large models (GPT-5.2, Claude 4.5 Opus) are slower than smaller models (GPT-4o-mini). For interactive applications, latency matters. Don't sacrifice user experience for marginal quality gains if speed is critical.

#### 5. Choosing based on name/reputation alone
Just because a model is "newest" or "most capable" doesn't mean it's right for your use case. GPT-5.2 is impressive, but GPT-4o-mini might be perfect for your needs at a fraction of the cost. Evaluate based on your requirements, not model names.

#### 6. Not considering context window requirements
If you need to process long documents (> 128K tokens), GPT-4o won't work regardless of quality. You need Claude 4.5 Sonnet (extended context), Gemini 3 Pro, or chunking strategies. Always check context window requirements.

#### 7. Sticking with one model forever
The model landscape evolves rapidly. New models launch, prices change, capabilities improve. Regularly re-evaluate your model choice. What was best 6 months ago might not be best today.

#### 8. Not using cost-effective models for high-volume tasks
For high-volume applications (thousands of requests per day), small cost differences add up quickly. Using GPT-4o-mini instead of GPT-4o can save thousands of dollars per month with minimal quality impact for many tasks.

#### 9. Ignoring provider-specific strengths
Different providers excel at different things. Anthropic's Claude models are excellent for code and analysis. Google's Gemini has great multimodal and long-context capabilities. OpenAI's GPT models are well-rounded. Don't limit yourself to one provider.

#### 10. Not implementing fallback strategies
Models can fail, hit rate limits, or become unavailable. Implement fallback strategies: try GPT-4o-mini first, fall back to GPT-4o if needed, or even switch providers (Claude, Gemini) if necessary. This improves reliability and can reduce costs.
